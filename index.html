The guiding purpose

Our Vision: The Restoration of America

This vision is dedicated to restoring an accountable, limited constitutional government that honors our Founding Fathers' enduring principles while building an American future defined by integrity, opportunity, and strength.

Government should serve the American people, uphold the rule of law, and remain accountable to the Constitution and the public trust.

Policy priorities

The Reform Agenda

Six pillars for a stronger, safer, more prosperous America.

1. Anti-corruption & lobbying reform

End improper influence, strengthen enforcement against corruption and insider trading, and hold public officials to clear ethical standards.

2. Fiscal integrity & tax reform

Simplify the tax code, reduce waste, and strengthen legislative oversight of public spending and taxpayer funds.

3. Constitutional limits

Review federal overreach and return appropriate authority to states, local communities, and citizens consistent with the Constitution, including the Tenth Amendment.

4. National & economic security

Secure the border, maintain modern defense capabilities, strengthen domestic manufacturing and supply chains, and address the cost of living.

5. Education & parent empowerment

Support parents and local communities, foundational skills, high standards, merit, and practical vocational training.

6. Infrastructure, energy & healthcare

Pursue energy independence, modern infrastructure, and patient-centered healthcare with greater price transparency and accountability.

A lesson from history

The Founders' Warnings

George Washington's 1796 Farewell Address warned Americans about the risks of factionalism, partisan passions, and foreign influence. Those warnings remain a prompt to place constitutional duty and the national interest above party loyalty.

“The great rule of conduct for us in regard to foreign nations is in extending our commercial relations, to have with them as little political connection as possible.”— GEORGE WASHINGTON, FAREWELL ADDRESS, 1796

A constitutional distinction

Standing for Office

I am standing for office—not running for office.

To stand for office is to offer oneself for public service as a duty: placing the Constitution, the rule of law, and the public trust above personal gain or partisan machinery. The office exists under the Constitution; the candidate is accountable to the people.

“The office pre-exists the candidate. To stand is to serve—not to seek power, but to fulfill a duty.”

— Luis J. Alford
